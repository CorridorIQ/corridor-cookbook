# Prep shapes

Offer the two or three that fit what you just read, recommend one, and say why in a sentence. The user can answer in their own words instead of picking.

| Shape | Fits when | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| One-page dossier | The user barely knows the people or the history | Who is in the room, the relationship so far, the state of play, what to watch for |
| Talking points | The user is driving the agenda | The three things to land, in order, with the line that lands each |
| Questions to ask | The user needs information more than airtime | Six questions ranked by what they would unlock, and why each matters |
| Negotiation read | Money, scope, or terms are on the table | What each side wants, what has already been conceded, the walk-away, the likely counter |
| Relationship refresher | A known contact after a long gap | Last contact, what was promised on both sides, personal details they mentioned, a natural opener |
| Decision brief | The meeting exists to decide something | The decision, the options, what is known, what is still missing, the recommendation |
| Status catch-up | A recurring internal check-in | What moved since last time, what slipped, what needs a call today |

Two shapes can combine when the meeting genuinely needs both, for example a decision brief with the questions that close its open items. Three is too many for one screen.
