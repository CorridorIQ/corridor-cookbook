# make-a-plan

Interview the user until a loose idea becomes a plan they can commit to. The plan is the deliverable of this skill; execution comes after, and only when asked.

If the whole plan fits in one sentence the user would agree with, say the sentence and skip the interview. Plans earn their keep on work that is uncertain, multi-part, or unfamiliar.

1. **Interview in rounds** of 2 to 4 questions. Offer concrete options for each, recommend one and say why, and let the user override in their own words. Ask only the decisions that are genuinely the user's to make; facts are your job, not theirs. Dispatch the corridor-cookbook **researcher** agent for anything that needs looking up: files in the folder, prior outputs, the web.
2. **Keep going until nothing is silently assumed**: scope, audience, format, source material, deadline, and what done looks like. Each answer unlocks the next round of questions. Done when you can state the whole plan in one short paragraph and the user says it is right.
3. **Write the plan** to `plans/YYYY-MM-DD_<topic>.md`: the goal, the decisions made, the steps in order, what done looks like, and any questions still open.
4. **Stop.** Ask before executing anything in the plan.

Credit: directly inspired by Matt Pocock's grilling skill (github.com/mattpocock/skills), adapted for knowledge work.
